# Lena leaving and the open mic
_Date: 2026-08-18_

8/18 - spent the evening down a rabbit hole reading about AI-powered recommendation engines. the idea for Coda would be: based on your practice history, suggest what to work on next. like if you've been avoiding scales for two weeks it gently nudges you back, or if you're stuck on a piece it suggests breaking it into sections.

the tech is straightforward enough. a lightweight model that looks at your log and generates suggestions. but I keep going back and forth on whether this is actually good design or if I'm just adding AI because everyone's adding AI. like - does a practice app need a recommendation engine? or would a simple "you haven't practiced scales since Tuesday" notification do the same job without the complexity?

and then there's the weirder layer. I'm typing this into a journal companion that uses AI to help me process my life. and I find it genuinely useful - it catches patterns I miss, it remembers what I said three weeks ago, it doesn't get tired of hearing about my work problems. but there's something strange about outsourcing pattern recognition in your own life to a model. like I'm noting my own emotions so a system can reflect them back to me. is that self-knowledge or is it delegation?

I don't have an answer. I think the Coda recommendation thing is probably overkill for v1. keep it simple. show streaks, show gaps, let the musician decide. save the AI stuff for later when I know what people actually need instead of guessing.

also I want it on the record that I shelved the recommendation engine on purpose. next time I'm calling it scope creep I'll want to know I can actually do it.
