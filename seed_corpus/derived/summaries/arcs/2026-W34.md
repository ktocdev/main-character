# Week 2026-W34 (2026-08-17 to 2026-08-21)

The week moved from private rehearsal to public performance, with Mika's characteristic maneuvering as the catalyst. On the 17th, Jordan practiced "Fast Car" for the open mic Mika had signed them up for without much warning — the familiar pattern of Mika deciding for Jordan what Jordan wouldn't decide alone. The practice session itself was a small joy, especially Pepper's off-key howling, and ended with a reminder Jordan noted directly: "I always forget that I like it until I do it."

Midweek brought a different kind of reflection. On the 18th, working through a recommendation-engine feature for Coda, Jordan spiraled into a broader question about AI-as-mirror — whether using a journal companion to catch patterns in one's own life counts as self-knowledge or is just delegation. No conclusion reached, but the instinct was toward restraint: keep Coda simple, hold off on AI bells and whistles until real user needs are known rather than assumed.

The 19th resolved a genuinely open logistical question: the lease. A solo run at Greenwood Park, capped with a new hill trail, became the setting for working through the math on moving versus renewing. Jordan decided to renew — the apartment, the neighborhood, Groundwork three blocks away, all now feel settled rather than provisional. The evening after (new rug, Pepper, Thai food, Motown documentary) was marked explicitly as the kind of contentment Jordan wants more of.

Work delivered a rare, clean win on the 20th: Marcus approved the dashboard direction with uncharacteristic ease, Lena backed it up with a thumbs-up and later a text that landed harder than the professional approval itself. Jordan named the shift that made this possible — designing from their own judgment instead of trying to guess Marcus's preferences. The day closed at Groundwork making real progress on Coda's quick-start feature, and Jordan allowed themselves a moment of "life approximately together."

The week's arc culminated on the 21st at the open mic itself. Jordan nearly backed out three times, but Mika's insistence (literally driving them there) forced follow-through. Dev's presence, and the easy overlap between Dev and Mika's tastes (Nigerian funk documentaries), added warmth. The performance was imperfect — thin opening, rough bridge — but real: a moment where self-consciousness dropped away mid-song. The aftermath (Dev's whistle, Mika's "I TOLD you," two hours lingering together) was framed as something Jordan wants to keep happening.

Unresolved: the Coda AI-design question remains genuinely undecided rather than settled, and the broader pattern — needing outside pressure (Mika, deadlines, Dev showing up) to do things Jordan values but wouldn't initiate — continues without comment on whether that's fine or worth examining.
