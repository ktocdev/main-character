# Creative — Jordan

**Where things stand:** Coda, the practice-tracking app for musicians that Jordan has been building on and off since before this record starts, is no longer a side project in the "someday" sense. It's the centerpiece of a portfolio case study that went out with a Northlight job application on 9/9, and it's the thing a hiring manager named Ana specifically dug into during a phone screen on 9/11 — she pulled up the write-up live on the call and asked pointed questions about the app's biggest design mistake. Onsite interview coming in about two weeks. Alongside the app, Jordan sang "Fast Car" at an open mic on 8/21, the first public performance mentioned in this record, and has kept doing music with Dev, a session bassist Jordan started seeing around the same time Coda picked back up steam.

**How it got here:** The Coda work tracks almost exactly against Jordan's stretches of work stress. 7/28 - Jordan starts logging real hours on it at Groundwork, a coffee shop that becomes the fixed location for all of this. Through July and August, the pattern repeats: something goes wrong at work, Jordan spirals, then eventually gets to Groundwork and builds something — the metronome (8/4), quick-start cards (8/12), a recommendation-engine detour that Jordan talked themselves out of as overkill (8/18). Two different people, Mika (7/31) and Lena (8/14), independently told Jordan that Coda could be the actual portfolio piece, months before Jordan believed them.

Dev entered the picture on 8/8 and became a real collaborator, not just a supportive friend — asking on 8/15 whether the app could track unstructured "noodling," which became the free-play mode Jordan prototyped in a breakthrough session on 8/25 (the timestamp-marker idea, arrived at while stirring coffee). The open mic thread runs alongside: Mika signed Jordan up without asking on 8/17, Jordan practiced with Pepper the cat howling along, and the 8/21 performance at The Lantern went imperfectly but landed — Dev and Mika both there, the three of them clicking as a group afterward.

The most important single entry for Coda is 9/3: Dev actually used the free-play feature for real, for a gig with a deadline, and revealed that Jordan had built a "trophy case" for collecting proof of feeling okay, when Dev needed a "bug tracker" for fixing mistakes before a show. Jordan had used the feature daily for nine days and never seen this. That reframe went straight into the case study that Ana responded to on the call.

**Open threads:** The fix for the free-play mode — making the marker neutral, letting the review step decide what a moment means — hasn't been built yet. The onsite with Northlight is still ahead, full day, four interviews plus a design-team presentation. Whether Dev actually uses Coda for the 9/19 gig, and what that reveals next, is unresolved. And there's the open question Jordan raised on 8/18 and never answered: whether running your own life through an AI journal companion is self-knowledge or delegation.
