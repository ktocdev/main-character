# Home

Jordan's living in a small one-bedroom that's just been re-upped for another year — signed the renewal on 9/14, one day before the September 15 deadline, with a $150 rent increase, running through October 31 of next year. It's not a dream apartment. The bedroom door doesn't close all the way, and a ceiling stain from an upstairs leak has been sitting there since at least early August, ignored by maintenance despite a photo and email submitted on 8/9. But it's Jordan's neighborhood now — Groundwork coffee shop three blocks away, Greenwood Park a ten-minute jog, everything mapped and familiar — and that ended up mattering more than the flaws.

The lease decision had been floating since Mom first asked about it on 8/7, during a call that also had Jordan looking at the apartment fresh through her eyes: the folding card table doing double duty as a dining table, the general smallness of it all. Jordan sat on the decision for weeks, avoiding it the way Jordan avoids things, until a solo run at Greenwood Park on 8/19 — full loop plus the hill trail, first time doing the extension — turned into the place where the math got done out loud: moving costs more than staying, the apartment is fine, the neighborhood is Jordan's. Decided on the trail, felt settled by the time dinner arrived that night.

Mom's actual visit came Labor Day weekend, 9/6. The ceiling stain was still unfixed, exactly as predicted. She approved of the new green rug (bought 8/1, immediately claimed by Pepper as prime real estate), brought an extra folding chair unprompted because Jordan had mentioned once in July only having two, and brought a box of Dad's old guitar picks — he doesn't play anymore, arthritis. She ate at the card table without a single comment about it. Pepper, who usually hides from guests, slept on her lap for an hour. It was also the visit where Jordan finally told her about the work situation — the restructuring, the whole thing — after sitting on it since July specifically to keep her from worrying. She wasn't angry, just sad she'd been kept out of it that long.

Domestic life in between has been small and steady: a real dinner instead of couch-eating on 8/1 (roasted chicken thighs, lemon, potatoes), the recurring bean-and-kale soup, laundry and vacuuming a rug that Pepper coats in fur within a day of cleaning. Pepper herself is a fixture throughout — knocking water glasses off nightstands at 2am, colonizing the counter she's banned from, curling up on the green rug with her white feet tucked under her, an unspoken arrangement of small rule-breaking that nobody enforces.

As of 9/14, the lease is signed and the apartment is settled for another year — even with a looming possibility that a new job (Northlight) could change the commute enough to need a car. Jordan decided not to let a job that doesn't exist yet dictate a housing decision that does.

Open threads: the ceiling stain, still unresolved. Maintenance's general unresponsiveness. Whether the commute situation actually changes once the Northlight outcome is known. And underneath all of it, the pattern Jordan named directly after Mom's visit — running information blackouts on the people who'd help, then telling the truth anyway and watching things get lighter — which home has quietly been a stage for all along.
