# Jordan's Journal Summary
**Updated August 9, 2026**

## Current Status
Jordan is a designer, 14 months into a job now facing a second reorg in as many years, with the internal tools team — the one they actually liked — at risk of dissolving in Q4. She lives with her cat Pepper in an apartment whose lease is up in October, a decision she's avoiding partly because it's tangled up with not knowing what her job will look like by then. Underneath the uncertainty, a real thread of good is running: Coda, a practice-tracking app side project, has gone from vague noodling to something with a working streak counter, a pulsing visual metronome, and now a first real external voice of interest (Dev, a musician). Jordan knows her own pattern cold — work blows up, she goes internal, cancels plans, punishes herself by sitting in it — and that self-knowledge hasn't yet stopped the mechanism, but the recovery time is visibly shortening.

## Self-Knowledge: Patterns & Truths
### Named this period — what became visible in these entries
- The collapse pattern is precise and self-aware: work hits (criticism, feeling unseen, feeling like nothing she makes matters) → Jordan goes internal → cancels, avoids contact, doesn't want to feel better yet, sits in it almost punitively. She named this outright on 8/3 and called it "not healthy" while still doing it.
- Leaving the apartment and making something — specifically at Groundwork — reliably breaks the fog, every time, and Jordan still doesn't skip straight there.
- The portfolio has been stalled since March; Coda stalling and restarting tracks almost exactly with work instability (per Mika's observation), suggesting the two aren't separate problems.

### Core truths — what holds across time
- Jordan cares deeply about her work and craft, and the wound isn't the criticism itself — it's that caring didn't matter to the person delivering it.
- Groundwork, Pepper, Mika, and now Coda's slow build are the reliable stabilizers; the couch and the phone-off silence are the reliable warning signs.
- Jordan doesn't need to "stop caring" like she imagines other people do — she's already recognized that most people who seem not to care are either hiding it or have paid a different cost for checking out.
