# Dev tries free play
_Date: 2026-09-03_

9/3 - Dev came over and used Coda for real for the first time. I sideloaded the build onto their phone and they stood in my living room with the bass and actually practiced for forty minutes while I pretended to read.

I've been running my own sessions through free play since I built it, nine days of it, so I thought I'd already found the rough edges. and I had found the obvious ones - the marker button started out this dinky 44pt thing and I made it a full-width strip across the bottom by day three, because you cannot look at a phone with a guitar in your lap. same with the auto-stop at 90 minutes, which killed a session on me once and never again.

what Dev found is not stuff I could have caught. they marked eleven moments. afterward we went through them together and every single one was a place where something went *wrong*. the fingering they keep flubbing. a transition that drags. a spot in the setlist where they lose the count. Dev said, totally matter-of-fact, "this is my list for tomorrow."

and I sat there realizing the entire review screen calls them highlights. the header literally says "your best moments." the export is called a highlight reel. I built a trophy case and Dev is using it as a bug tracker.

it's not that one of us is wrong. it's that I never modeled a person who practices toward a deadline. Dev has a gig on the 19th and a setlist and a bandleader. their practice is triage. I practice when I'm stressed from work. I mark the moments where something finally clicked because that's the whole reason I picked up the guitar that night. I built a feature that collects evidence that I'm okay, as a way to feel better about myself.

the fix is probably not two modes. it's that the app shouldn't name the marks at all until you tell it what they are. let the mark be neutral and let the review step ask.

at mark six Dev said "oh - I do that same run every time I don't know what to play." ninety seconds into looking at their own data they found a crutch they'd had for years. that's the app working, just not the way I intended.

then they played the thing they'd marked as the worst one, straight through, four times, until it wasn't. Pepper left the room at the high slapped notes and came back at the end like she'd made her position clear.
