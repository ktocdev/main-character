write — the journal companion

One chat stays open for as long as it feels right — about a week in practice. The write screen is that whole conversation: the chat it continues, your entries, the companion's replies, and everything you say back. save entry stores a journal entry (backed up to a markdown file the moment you save); send talks with the companion about it — both join the same chat and both become journal memory at close. Your draft autosaves as you type and survives a refresh or closed tab; Enter just makes a new line, only the buttons send.

When the chat feels finished, open the ⋯ menu next to the composer and hit summarize & close chat (also available on the history tab). That's the summarize point: your side of the chat becomes a journal entry — the same shape as your imported conversations — and tagging, entity extraction, summaries, and dream scanning all run in the background. A fresh empty chat opens. Until then, new entries live in the conversation (and their markdown backups), not yet in long-term memory.

Dreams need no flag: write them the way you always have ("last night I had a dream…") and the dream extractor finds them at the summarize point, pulling each one into the dream realm while the entry itself stays in waking memory — exactly how your imported history behaves.

chat — look things up

The chat screen is a tool, not the journal: ask it to pull things out of your history ("when did I last see Sam?", "what did I say about the move?"). It runs the same retrieval + companion brain, but it's a separate conversation — lookups never join the open chat and never become journal memory. clear wipes it whenever you like. Note its honest limit: it sees retrieved excerpts, not the whole journal, so answers are representative rather than exhaustive.

search — every match, instantly

The search screen sweeps your entire journal locally — free, instant, no API calls. meaning mode ranks passages by how semantically close they are to whatever you describe, in your own words ("times I felt proud of my work"); exact text mode finds literal matches, newest first, with a ×N count when an entry contains several. Click any result to read the full entry in place. This is the exhaustive counterpart to the chat screen: chat retrieves a handful of excerpts and reasons about them, search shows you everything. Dreams never appear here — they live in their own realm (dreams tab). Two honest limits: highlighting is literal (a typo'd word won't light up even though meaning-search still finds the right passages), and dates are the conversation's date — a passage written mid-week inside a week-long chat shows that chat's date until the Phase 3 per-day split.

history — every chat, one at a time

Lands on the open chat: the conversation it continues (with Claude's replies interleaved, backfilled from your export), your entries, and the live thread. Every closed chat sits in the sidebar — click one to read it in full. Closed chats keep both sides for reading, but only your side is journal memory.

entities — curation workflow, the efficient order

1 find duplicates first entities tab → "find duplicates (free)"

Runs locally in a few seconds, no API cost. Work through every pair: merge the real ones (either direction — pick the name you actually use), hit not duplicates for the rest. Dismissals are permanent, so this list only ever shrinks. Doing merges first matters because every merge shrinks the triage queue you're about to walk.

2 optional: ask claude entities tab → "ask claude: people / projects / places"

Catches conceptual duplicates the local scan can't — long descriptive names that mean the same project, that kind of thing. Costs one API call per kind. Worth one pass on projects especially. Apply or dismiss each group; nothing merges without your click.

3 live in triage until the queue is empty triage tab

Entities come one at a time, junk first (1-mention entities lead). Keep your hands on the keyboard:

kkeep — marks reviewed ✓, next. Your default key.
ddelete — junk, noise, things that shouldn't exist. No confirm; u undoes.
mmerge into another entity (autocomplete; old name kept as alias)
ccorrect — typo fix; old name is not kept as alias (Dana → Dane)
rrename — fix spelling or case of this entity's own name
aalias — add another name this entity goes by (keeps it matchable by both)
t then 1/2/3retype to person / project / place
sskip — decide later, stays in queue for next time
uundo last change (50 deep, works across sessions)

Most entities are a 1-second k or d. Don't deliberate on skips — that's what s is for. Every key also has a matching on-screen button if you're mousing; the ones that need a target (merge, correct, rename, alias) open a field with apply / cancel buttons — Enter and Esc still work. Undo/redo buttons live on the entities screen (they cover triage actions too).

4 deep fixes on the tricky ones entities tab → click entity → hover an observation

For entities that are conflations — two Sams in one node, "karaoke bar" that's really two different bars — use the observation tools: move reassigns a single memory to the right entity (type person:Sam (work) to create a new one), edit fixes wrong facts, × removes them. These are the slow, careful fixes — save them for after the fast passes so you're not context-switching.

5 ongoing habit

Memory now updates at the summarize point — closing a chat tags it, extracts its entities, refreshes the week's arc, its summary, and the domain documents, scans it for dreams, and integrates the chat into a seed summary candidate — the rolling co-edited life summary. A banner on the write tab announces it: download the candidate there, read it fully, edit it, and upload it back: the uploaded file is the seed every chat opens with, and nothing automatic ever overwrites it. detect patterns in the patterns tab still re-reads everything for new recurring shapes when you ask. Closed chats create new entities that arrive unreviewed, so an occasional minute in triage keeps the graph clean forever. You never redo work — reviewed marks persist, and category fixes (× a wrong tag in the categories tab) stick the same way entity merges do.

categories

Every entry gets tagged with categories (work, relationships, health…) — automatically when a chat closes; tag new entries re-runs it manually for anything untagged (already-tagged entries are cached and free). Click a category chip to browse its entries and read its domain document — a living ~500-word summary of that area of life. × a wrong tag or + a missing one; your fixes are permanent overrides. scan for new categories clusters your places/projects and proposes organic categories — nothing is created without your confirm. You can also hand-seed a category with keywords. Entry categories are deliberately flat — broad and scannable — so there's no sub-category nesting here.

patterns

detect patterns re-reads the weekly arcs + domain documents and names recurring shapes — emotional cycles, behavioral pipelines, relationship dynamics — each with dated instances and a confidence level. Patterns are injected into the companion's context only when the conversation genuinely rhymes with one. dismiss hides a pattern; it only returns if new evidence shows up.

dreams

Dreams live in their own realm: a separate collection that waking questions can never accidentally surface. The extractor finds dreams inside your entries (at the summarize point, or via extract dreams from history), keeping the narrative, cast, tones, and — only if you gave one — your own interpretation. Recent dream tone shows up as the dream weather line. Asking the companion about dreams crosses the boundary explicitly.

safety notes

Everything is undoable — ↺/↻ in the nav cover every curation and observation change, 50 steps back. All your decisions live in entity_graph/curation.json and re-apply on every rebuild, so nothing you do here is ever lost by the system regenerating docs. One exception to know: a --force re-extraction (a from-scratch API re-read of all entries, only ever run deliberately) rebuilds the raw memory files and would discard observation-level edits — merges, renames and the rest survive even that. Removed aliases keep their historical "as ‘name’" data; removal only stops name-matching. And your journal is these local folders: chroma_data, journal_entries, entity_graph, summaries — back them up.
